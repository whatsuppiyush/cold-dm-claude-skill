# DM Playbook — cold DMs on X that get replies

The whole game is this: send a message a real person is glad to receive. Everything below serves
that. If a tactic would make the recipient roll their eyes, don't do it — a bad DM costs a send, a
bit of account trust, and any chance with that lead.

## The first DM

**Structure (2–4 sentences, no more):**

1. **A true, specific hook.** Reference something real about *them* — a line from their bio, a post
   they shipped, a problem they tweeted about, a milestone. Specificity is proof you're not blasting.
   - Weak: "Hey Jane, love your content!"
   - Strong: "Jane — saw you just moved Sparrow off Mailchimp and were ranting about deliverability.
     Been there."
2. **One reason you're reaching out.** Connect their situation to what you do, in one line. Not a
   feature list — the outcome that matters to them.
3. **A low-friction ask.** A question or a soft "worth a look?" — never "book a demo" in message #1.
   The goal of DM #1 is a *reply*, not a meeting.

**Rules:**
- **No links in the first DM** unless the user asks. Links tank reply rates and trip spam heuristics.
- **No `{merge_field}` feel.** "Hi {name}, I saw {company} is in {industry}" reads as automated. Write
  each hook from the actual profile. If you can't find a genuine hook, drop the lead — don't pad with
  generic filler.
- **No hashtags, no emoji spam, no walls of text.** One emoji at most, and only if it fits.
- **Match their register.** An indie hacker and a VP of Sales get different tones.
- **Ask one thing.** Two questions halve the reply rate.

## Personalization depth

Tiers, cheapest to richest:

- **Bio-only** (free, already in the lead row): fine for volume, weakest hook. Pull one specific noun
  from the bio.
- **Recent-tweet** (5 credits to fetch their tweets if you don't have them): much stronger — reference
  something they said this week. Worth it for higher-value leads.
- **Engagement-based** (they replied to / retweeted a specific tweet — a premium lead source): the
  richest, because you know exactly what they care about right now. Lead with that shared context.

Spend richer personalization on the leads most worth winning; use bio-only for the long tail. Never
fake a detail — if you didn't see it in their profile or tweets, don't claim it.

## Follow-ups

- **1–2 follow-ups, not five.** Space them **2–4 days** apart. Each must add a *new* angle (a
  different benefit, a relevant example, a genuine question) — never "just bumping this."
- **Stop the instant they reply.** Check the conversation before sending any queued follow-up; a
  reply moves them to the inbox flow, not the next sequence step.
- **A soft breakup** as the last touch works: "No worries if it's not a fit — I'll leave you to it.
  If <specific problem> ever gets loud, I'm around." It often gets the reply the pitch didn't.

## Handling replies

- **Interested / "tell me more":** answer briefly, then make the ask concrete — a specific time, a
  short link, a yes/no. Don't bury them in a pitch.
- **A question:** answer it plainly and honestly first; earn the next step. Don't dodge into a demo.
- **Objection:** acknowledge it, give one honest counter, don't argue. If it's a real no, respect it.
- **Not interested / negative:** thank them, stop, and add them to the Do-Not-Contact list. A clean
  exit protects your reputation and the account.
- **Auto-reply / noise:** ignore, don't count it as a reply.

## What "success" means (and how to report it)

Measure **replies and conversations started**, and ultimately **calls booked** — never raw DM send
counts. A campaign that sends fewer, better DMs and starts more conversations beats a bigger blast
every time. Report it that way to the user, too.
